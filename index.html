Methodology

How the index is built

The index turns live driving times into one honest number per city: how many minutes it currently takes to cover one kilometre, on average, along that city's busiest cross-city corridors.

1

Fix eight border points

Every city gets eight reference points sitting roughly at its outer limits: North, South, East, West, North-East, North-West, South-East and South-West — typically toll plazas, checkposts or well-known junctions at the city boundary.

2

Drive four corridors, not eight legs

The eight points form four opposite pairs. We pull live, traffic-aware driving distance and duration for each pair:

  • East → West
  • North → South
  • North-East → South-West
  • North-West → South-East

Together these four corridors cut through the city centre from every compass direction, giving a fair read on congestion regardless of which side of town you're on.

3

Pull live traffic, not the timetable time

Each request is sent with a departure time of "now," which forces Google's routing engine to return traffic-aware duration rather than free-flow base time. This is what makes the index reflect what a driver would actually experience right now.

4

Convert to one number: minutes per kilometre

We add up the distance of all four corridors and the traffic-aware time it takes to drive them, then divide time by distance:

Index (min/km) = Total travel time (min) ÷ Total distance (km)

A city where the index reads 4.5 means: on average, expect roughly four-and-a-half minutes for every kilometre travelled along its main cross-city routes right now. Lower is better — it means traffic is moving freely.

5

Refresh every hour

A scheduled job re-runs the calculation for every city once an hour and republishes the results, so the index tracks how congestion builds through the morning and evening peaks and eases overnight.

Reading the congestion bands

Free flow — under 2.5 min/km
Moderate — 2.5 to 4 min/km
Heavy — 4 to 6 min/km
Severe — above 6 min/km
Honest limitations. Border points approximate a city's outer limits and are periodically reviewed as cities grow — they are reference markers, not administrative boundaries. Because the index averages just four corridors, it describes overall cross-city conditions, not any single street or your specific route. Treat it as a city-level pulse check, not a substitute for live navigation before you drive.
